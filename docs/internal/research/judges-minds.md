## 1. Illia Polosukhin — NEAR co-founder

**Role in this hackathon:** NEAR co-founder and the jury's home-team judge. His October 2026 builder-ideas post is the single most load-bearing taste document in this file — it is, in effect, a menu of shapes he has already pre-approved.

### What he has publicly praised / proposed (VERIFIED via search snippets; the underlying X thread itself is PROVISIONAL — see BROWSER-REQUEST)

On 3 Oct 2026 Illia published an X post of project ideas for developers, framed by the observation that as building gets easier, "what to build" and distribution matter more than "how to build" (close paraphrase of reporting).
Sources:
- https://www.panews.io/articles/01a10089-6287-74d0-8901-28ee5b531355
- https://crypto.news/near-co-founder-pitches-ai-pets-private-trading/
- https://www.odaily.news/en/newsflash/522307

The ideas reported across those three reports (all three agree on the core list; wording below is paraphrase, not Illia's verbatim text):

1. **Verifiable AI Tamagotchi (AI Tamagochi in the reports):** an NFT character that carries its own NEAR balance to pay for AI inference, with NEAR AI running verifiable inference from a prompt encoded on-chain; feed/dress mechanics, then battle arenas and competitions.
2. **Universal Checkout:** a component supporting payments from any wallet and any chain (via NEAR Intents).
3. **AI encyclopedia as prediction markets:** facts become ongoing markets (Augur-style); AI generates pages from the markets' current assessment of each fact, rather than community editing.
4. **Private OTC market on NEAR Intents.**
5. **Position management that avoids liquidation** — automated strategies that rebalance via Intents so the user is never liquidated; delta-neutral strategies are listed separately in the same reports.
6. **Private code review** (fully private).
7. **Private copy/social trading.**
8. **Private AI second-opinion medical consultations.**
9. **Private voice transcription.**
10. **AI model evaluation against private test sets** — certified results tied to a model and the test-set hash, without exposing the dataset.
11. **Escrow for event-registration funds** (cross-chain event escrow in the task brief's wording; the reports say "escrow for event registration funds").
12. **Company equity and fundraising management.**

Distribution advice reported in the same post: distribute through NEAR Legion, Aurora Intent Connect, other communities, Product Hunt, and X.
Source: https://www.panews.io/articles/01a10089-6287-74d0-8901-28ee5b531355

### His wider stated thesis (VERIFIED via search snippets)

- "AI is the front end and blockchain markets is the back end": agents that hire other agents, pay for private AI, and buy/sell/ship for businesses on-chain; AI agents will become blockchain's main users, with wallets/blockchain abstracted away until invisible to humans (close paraphrase of interview reporting).
Sources:

### Product shapes he demonstrably likes

- **Agent-owned money:** an asset/account that holds its own balance and spends it on inference/compute without a human in the loop (the Tamagotchi is the purest form).
- **Intents as the settlement layer:** cross-chain execution where the user states the outcome and Intents route/settle it; checkout, OTC, and never-liquidated rebalancing are all Intents-native shapes.
- **Confidentiality as a feature, not a toggle:** private trading, private review, private medical opinion, private transcription — privacy is the product's reason to exist on NEAR rather than on a plain web backend.
- **Verifiability where trust is the bottleneck:** verifiable inference, certified private-benchmark results — a receipt the user can check, not a claim.

### What he has publicly dismissed / scored poorly

- **No public signal found** for a specific Illia "I dislike X" list in the sources searched. Do not attribute a generic dismissal (e.g. "he hates memecoins") to him. The nearest negative signal is structural, not personal: his Oct 2026 framing implies the failure mode he expects is *building the wrong thing or failing at distribution* ("what to build and how to gain user distribution"), not failing at implementation difficulty. An entry that is technically fine but has no distribution story and no user is, on his own framing, the miss — but that is inference from his framing, labelled as such.

### Implication for a 2026 entry targeting Illia

Build one of his named shapes, literally, on Intents + NEAR AI Cloud/TEE where the shape calls for it, with the agent/account holding and spending its own balance in the demo — and ship a distribution path (Legion / Intent Connect / Product Hunt / X) in the README, not just code. A Tamagotchi-style agent with its own balance, a universal checkout widget, or a private-OTC / never-liquidated strategy are the lowest-friction ways to be legible to him. Do not pitch him a generic chain app that happens to run on NEAR; pitch him a NEAR-native shape he already asked for.

### INFERENCE — what "AI judge" evaluation likely rewards (labelled inference, not hackathon statements)

*Basis for the inference (stated so it can be checked): an AI judge can only reward what it can verify from the repo + demo link without human intuition — runnable steps, expected outputs, named primitives, and falsifiable claims. A human judge can forgive a broken demo step if the idea is exciting; a machine reader that is told to follow "do X, see Y" cannot, because a failed step is the whole signal it has.*

- **Likely rewarded:** a README that is parseable in one pass (problem → product → how NEAR powers it → exact run steps → expected result per step); every claim tied to a file, command, transaction, or screenshot; named NEAR primitives (Intents, Chain Signatures, TEE/AI Cloud, function-call keys, sub-accounts, meta-transactions) with the exact place each is used; a demo video/screenshots that show the claimed outcome actually happening; tests or a re-runnable check (the `thesis` track's "models anyone can re-run" hint generalises well here).
- **Likely punished:** marketing copy with no steps; "do X" steps whose "see Y" does not happen on a fresh checkout; a private or broken repo link; claims of integrations that are not in the code; a token/mechanic described but not demonstrable; a README that buries the run instructions under narrative.
